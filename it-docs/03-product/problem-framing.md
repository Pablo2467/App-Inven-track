# 03 — Problem Framing

## The problem

Inventory movements are often recorded manually or in separate records, making it difficult to maintain an accurate view of available stock. When products enter the inventory at different times, manually determining which stock should leave first can also lead to incorrect FIFO handling.

This can cause stock discrepancies, unnecessary manual work, and difficulty tracing how the current inventory level was reached.

## Who is affected

| Group | How |
|---|---|
| Inventory staff | Record entries and exits manually and must calculate available stock |
| Store employees | May not have an up-to-date view of available products |
| Store manager | Has difficulty verifying current inventory and movement history |
| Business owner | May face stock discrepancies and inefficient inventory control |

## Cost of doing nothing

Continuing to manage inventory manually increases the risk of incorrect stock quantities, incorrect FIFO application, and time spent checking previous records to determine the current inventory state.

Older stock may remain in the inventory while newer stock is removed first, reducing the reliability of the inventory process.

## Success criterion

The problem is solved when:

- Every stock entry and stock exit is recorded in Iven-Track.
- Available stock can be determined from the recorded inventory movements.
- Every stock exit follows the FIFO rule for the corresponding product.
- A user can identify the inventory movements associated with a product without relying on separate manual records.
